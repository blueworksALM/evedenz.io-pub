The gap

Cloud ALM tracks the work. Auditors ask for the proof.

In a regulated (GxP) setting, every change to a system needs a documented, signed trail: what was specified, who approved it, what was tested, and whether that approval still covers what went live. Out of the box, that evidence ends up in spreadsheets, PDFs on shared drives and email threads beside your ALM tool.

  • Signatures live outside the toolApprovals are collected by email or on paper and copied back by hand.
  • Nobody knows if a signature still appliesThe requirement changed after sign-off. Does the approval still count?
  • Workflow steps aren't gatedA feature can move to testing or production without the sign-off the SOP requires.
  • Reports are assembled before every auditSomeone rebuilds the feature's history from five different apps.
What you get

Validation evidence, built into the screens your teams already use.

Evedenz injects its controls into the Cloud ALM launchpad. Features get a signatures section, a validation section and a timeline, with no separate app to learn.

Qualified e-signatures

Sign feature reports through Skribble at the level each step needs: simple (SES), advanced (AES) or qualified (QES). Signed PDFs and signing protocols are kept with the feature.

Signature gates

Define per transition which signatures are required. A feature can't move to the next workflow status until its sign-offs are complete.

"In Validation" step

A dedicated validation stage on top of specification, with intended-use classification, change type, target system and business process, picked from value helps.

Complete feature reports

One PDF covering the feature with its requirements, user stories, tasks, defects, documents and references, including document content and change history.

Risk linkage

Link Cloud ALM risks to features. Impact, probability, mitigation and owner go into the signed report and count toward change detection.

Audit timeline

Requests, signatures, changes, withdrawals and invalidations are recorded as events, so the history of every approval can be read in one place.

Signature validity

A signature means what it meant when it was given.

Each signature stores a snapshot of everything its report covered. Evedenz compares that snapshot with the live feature every time the feature is opened, and applies three clear rules.

Signed

Valid and current

Nothing the report covered has changed since signing. The signature counts for gates and as proof.

Changed since

Valid, newer version exists

A change never invalidates a signature. Evedenz flags that the signed version is no longer the latest and lists exactly what changed. The next workflow gate asks for fresh confirmation.

Invalid

The step was undone

When a feature is set back before the status a signature was given at, the step it authorised no longer stands. The record stays visible with its reason, PDF and protocol. Open requests are withdrawn automatically.

Example: a feature steps back from "Successfully Tested" to "In Implementation"

Both the handover and the test confirmation are invalidated, and the gates ask for them again. The specification approval still stands.

Signed reports

A document a signer can read, and an auditor can trust.

The report is rendered once, when the request is sent. The signer approves exactly those bytes. No preview drift, no "the PDF I signed looked different".

  • States its purpose. Who requested it, which record and status it belongs to, which signature quality was asked for.
  • Describes what it covers. Every linked task and document in full, with specification content rendered as text.
  • Says when something is empty. "No process risks are assigned" is a statement, not a missing section.
  • One reference throughout. The report ID appears on every page and ties the record, protocol, events and signature together.

How it runs

Your tenant stays untouched. Your data stays in your subaccount.

Evedenz sits in front of your Cloud ALM tenant as a secure proxy. Users open the Evedenz URL instead of the tenant URL, log in as usual through your identity provider, and see Cloud ALM with Evedenz built in.

No tenant modification

Cloud ALM is used through its public APIs and a proxied launchpad. There's nothing to install on the tenant and nothing to remove if you stop.

Your identity provider

The login round trip through SAP Identity Authentication and your corporate IdP (for example Microsoft Entra) works as it does today.

Role-based access

An SAP Application Router enforces User and Admin roles per route with XSUAA. Without a role, every page answers 403.

Your database

Signatures, snapshots, PDFs and events are stored in PostgreSQL in your subaccount, in their own schema, so an existing instance can be shared.

Admin setup page

Configure signature policies, Cloud ALM API credentials and launchpad tiles from one page, with a built-in API access test.

Tile manager

Hide launchpad tiles your teams don't need and add your own, such as a link to SOPs or the Evedenz setup page.

FAQ

Questions we hear often

Does Evedenz change anything in our Cloud ALM tenant?

No. Evedenz runs as a separate application in your SAP BTP subaccount and talks to Cloud ALM through its public APIs. Its own data (signatures, validation classification, risk links, settings) is stored in its own database.

Which signature levels are supported?

Through Skribble you can request simple (SES), advanced (AES) and qualified (QES) electronic signatures. You choose the required level per workflow step.

What happens to a signature if the requirement changes afterwards?

It stays valid, and is marked "Changed since" with a list of exactly what changed. Fresh confirmation comes with the next workflow gate. A signature only becomes invalid when the step it authorised is undone, or when an admin revokes it with a reason.

What exactly does a signature cover?

The feature's fields and change history, its linked tasks and their documents (including document content), references and transports, its validation classification and its assigned risks. The workflow status itself is excluded, because the signature exists to authorise that transition.

Can we try it without connecting a tenant?

Yes. A demo mode runs with a sample launchpad, fixture data and a simulated signing service, so you can walk through the whole signature lifecycle before connecting anything.
